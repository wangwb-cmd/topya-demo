1. What this notice covers

This notice concerns the Android VPN application presented as Halo. It describes the permissions and app data flows confirmed in the available product materials. It does not claim that the VPN service is independently audited, that no operational records exist, or that every server-side process has been inspected.

2. Android permissions and your choices

To establish a VPN tunnel, Android asks you to approve the system VPN connection request. If you decline, the app cannot establish that tunnel. The app may separately ask for notification permission so Android can display connection status. Declining notifications does not itself grant VPN approval and does not prevent you from choosing whether to approve a later VPN request.

The region picker records the selected VPN location on this device so a valid choice can be restored. Halo's connection history is stored locally on the device; the design keeps up to 20 recent entries. Clearing app storage or uninstalling may remove that local history. It is not represented as a cloud backup.

3. Account and service operation

The reviewed app flow creates or uses a guest service session even when no email sign-in screen is shown. The service exchanges configuration and node information with its backend to populate connection choices and operate the connection. Do not interpret the absence of an email field as an anonymous connection with no service-side identifier.

4. Connection diagnostics

Application reporting paths identified in the reviewed materials can submit operational records, subject to app configuration and runtime behavior. Documented fields include a guest or user level, a device fingerprint, device details, region, VPN exit IP, selected node and routing context, connection or disconnection outcomes, and advertising events or diagnostics. Some records are queued for native submission; a local submission call does not prove backend receipt.

No visited-domain or browsing-history field was identified in the reviewed connection-reporting calls. That limited code review is not a guarantee that no traffic metadata is processed elsewhere in the VPN infrastructure, operating system, ad services, or network. This service does not make a blanket “zero logs” promise.

5. Advertising and service providers

The app contains an advertising orchestration path identified as TopOn and an advertising-event reporter identified as AdProfitHub. Configuration may cause an advertising SDK to initialize or request an ad at different points in a connection flow; an ad is not guaranteed on every attempt. Advertising and analytics providers may receive technical or interaction data according to the SDKs actually included and enabled in the released build. Before release, the operator needs to verify the provider list against the shipped package and disclose each recipient, the information it handles, and its privacy notice.

6. Retention, security, and rights

Local Halo history follows the device-storage behavior described above. The available materials do not establish server diagnostic retention periods, deletion procedures, processing countries, or a verified security-certification scope. The operator needs to document those practices and provide a working route for legally available access, correction, and erasure requests.

7. Children and changes

The service is not designed as a child-directed product. The operator should add the age rule and response process that apply in the markets where the app is offered. If material data practices change, the operator should update this notice and identify the effective date.
