Privacy Policy

Pulse VPN · Updated 2 November 2026

1. When this policy applies

This policy covers the Pulse VPN Android app. Pulse presents available locations, lets you choose a route, and shows whether Android has established a VPN tunnel. The information described here supports those activities and the app's troubleshooting flow.

2. System permissions

A VPN connection requires your approval in Android's system dialog. Refusing the dialog prevents Pulse from opening the tunnel. Pulse may request notification permission separately to show connection status; refusing notifications is not the same decision as refusing VPN approval.

3. Route preference and guest access

Pulse may keep a valid selected region on your device for the next session. The app also uses a guest service session and requests service configuration and location data from its backend. You can use a guest session without entering an email address, although a service identifier may still be used.

4. Connection diagnostics

To understand tunnel creation, disconnection, and route problems, Pulse can submit diagnostic information according to its configuration. The information may include device fingerprint and characteristics, guest or user level, selected region and node, VPN egress IP, routing context, connection results, and ad events. Some submissions are handed to a native reporting path. Pulse does not describe this as a guarantee that no traffic metadata is processed elsewhere in the network.

5. Reports you start

Pulse includes an on-device troubleshooting view and a user-initiated report action. If you choose to use that action, review the information presented in the app before sending it. Background connection diagnostics can operate independently of a manual report.

6. Advertising

The app's advertising flow uses a TopOn-named coordinator and an AdProfitHub-named event reporter. Depending on connection state and configuration, an enabled advertising service may receive technical or interaction details for loading ads, measuring events, and diagnosing failures. Ads can be skipped in some flows.

7. Device controls and changes

You may change the saved location and manage app data using Android's controls. You can also withhold VPN authorization or notifications. If the information practices of Pulse change materially, this policy will be revised.

Terms of Service

Pulse VPN · Updated 2 November 2026

1. Agreement to use Pulse

These terms govern the Pulse VPN Android service. By using Pulse, you agree to them. If you disagree, do not begin a connection session.

2. Selecting and verifying a route

Pulse lets you review available regions and choose a route. A saved selection can be restored on your device when valid. A route selection or diagnostic reading is not confirmation of a connected tunnel; the app relies on the VPN service state for that status.

3. Time and traffic

The product design provides a new session of up to 90 minutes without a per-session data-volume limit. Switching nodes during a session is intended to preserve the remaining time. Actual operation depends on the available configuration and network conditions.

4. Android authorization

You control the system VPN approval. Without it, Pulse cannot create a tunnel. You are responsible for maintaining your device and internet access. Notification permission serves a different purpose and does not authorize the VPN.

5. Advertising events

Pulse may request or display advertising as part of a connection flow. An ad may be unavailable, and neither an ad request nor ad viewing ensures a successful VPN connection.

6. Lawful behavior

Do not use the app to violate the law, infringe rights, disrupt another user or network, spread harmful software, or bypass access controls. Do not test or access Pulse systems without authorization, except where a legal right prevents that restriction.

7. Service changes
